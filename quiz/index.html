Tava — The Outbreak Decision

This caselet works through a QSR chain's crisis response during a citywide flu outbreak using a simple four-step framework: understand the problem, identify the options, evaluate the economics, and decide with the long term in mind.

Not every option deserves equal weight. Focus first on what's actually feasible once the constraints are known.

Understand the Problem → Identify the Options → Evaluate the Economics → Decide for the Long Term
  • What options does Tava have? Narrow down to what's actually feasible once regulations are known.
  • Which option makes the most business sense? Compare the financial impact of each option against the realistic alternative.
  • What does the decision mean beyond the crisis? Consider the long-term value the decision could create.

ConceptWhat you learnt
Scenario AnalysisComparing different possible options or outcomes to understand which choice creates the most value under a given set of assumptions.
Unit EconomicsEvaluating revenue and costs at the level of one outlet, customer or transaction to understand whether a business model is economically viable.
Fixed vs. Variable CostsFixed costs generally remain unchanged with short-term changes in activity, while variable costs change with activity — important when deciding whether to shut down or continue operating.
Sensitivity AnalysisTesting how a recommendation changes when an important assumption, such as the level of revenue decline, changes.
Pilot TestingTesting a new business model or capability on a limited scale, learning from actual results, and using that evidence before making a broader decision.
